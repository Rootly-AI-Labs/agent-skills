# Audience-specific drafts

Start with the internal fact list: claim, recorded action phase where applicable,
source, and unknowns. Then write the recipient draft, reusing the audience-approved
action wording, estimate conditions and update commitment from that list. Keep this
verification list separate from the text intended for recipients.
Show draft status, audience, proposed destination, observation time, and required
review. Every draft includes known impact, unknowns, work underway, recovery
estimate or explicit unknown, and next update or pending approval. Unrecorded
work stays unknown, including when the recorded status is "investigating";
do not turn that label into "our team is investigating."
Use the source's observation timestamp. If only retrieval time is known, label
it as retrieval time, not measurement time; otherwise mark time unavailable.
Never infer an observation interval from incident start and next-update times.
A recorded incident start is not proof of impact onset or the time responders
confirmed it. Attribute that timestamp as recorded metadata, or omit it from
customer text unless impact evidence supports the same time.

## Responders / commander handoff

Use a concise current-state brief, metric values/trends, hypotheses labeled as
unconfirmed/checked/ruled out, and ordered actions with assigned or proposed
owners clearly distinguished. Commander handoff adds current decisions,
unresolved checks, and the acknowledgment needed for transfer.

## Executives

Describe who is affected and how badly, external messages already sent, a plain
summary, next steps, and a sourced mitigation range when available. Suggest the
company's direct briefing channel. Do not post or add executives to the bridge.

## Customers

Use approved templates where required. Describe degraded features and confirmed
scope in plain language. Keep cause hypotheses internal. If no defensible
recovery range exists, say it is not yet known; do not fill a template with a
made-up estimate. Keep verification notes, internal IDs, and speculation outside
the recipient text. Obtain human review before any external publication workflow.

## Final accuracy check

Would every factual sentence remain accurate if the leading hypothesis were
wrong? Are estimate conditions and next-update commitments actually supported?
Can each claimed action and timestamp be traced to the supplied evidence?
Are data-safety or recovery assurances corroborated rather than inferred?
Revise the draft to pass these checks before returning it. Keep requests for
missing evidence or new commitments in reviewer notes, not as unsupported copy
with a warning asking the reviewer to remove it.
