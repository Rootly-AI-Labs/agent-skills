# Responder judgment

Generated from `tools/templates/responder-judgment.md`. Read when recommending extra help,
coverage changes, or interpreting an individual's workload, not for a simple
"who is on call?" lookup.

First explain why extra help is needed: missing expertise, service ownership or
decision authority, stalled mitigation, expanding impact, or a stated need for
help. Then identify a suitable responder using service knowledge, required
access, relevant on-call duties, and explicit availability. Seniority alone is
not proof of the needed expertise or authority.

Useful load signals include active incidents, page priorities and actionability,
the shift window, and documented consecutive nights of paging. Only use
person-set PTO, out-of-office, availability status, or an explicit request for
help as availability evidence. Read only calendar information authorized for
the task; avoid exposing meeting titles or personal details unnecessarily.
Raw page counts, Slack presence, response speed, and guessed working hours are
not measures of suitability. Raw or deduplicated page counts alone establish
neither overload nor light workload. Never infer illness or personal circumstances from
messages. A tool's health score is a signal to contextualize, not a diagnosis.

Explain the operational reason and cited signals for any responder suggestion.
A schedule lookup does not prove calendar availability, skill, access, consent,
or adequate capacity. Human agreement is required for any proposed coverage.
